# Overview

Owner, 2026-10-31: "the expose hot corner doesnt work, and the expose looks
bugged imo i want it like niri's overview." HyprExpo's grid goes. The shell
draws the overview itself, the way niri does (YaLTeR/niri wiki, Overview):
the workspaces zoomed out and stacked vertically, each one a strip of its
windows at their own places in the layout, the current one centred, the
bar above it all. It is ours, so it follows DESIGN.md and the motion
clocks, and it works the same under mango.

## 1. The hot corner

Two causes, both on the owner's hosts:

- **A corner on a shared edge can never be hit.** g815's eDP-1 sits right
  of HDMI-A-1 with its top edge level with HDMI-A-1's, so the pointer
  leaving eDP-1's top-left corner walks into HDMI-A-1 instead of stopping
  in a 4px square at x=2560. A corner gets a surface only where the
  desktop really ends on both of its edges, the way GNOME places its hot
  corner: no other output's box holds the point one pixel past it
  horizontally, vertically or diagonally.
- **The corner waited on a plugin.** The overview corner was mapped only
  once the compositor had answered that HyprExpo was loaded; a shell that
  started first (e1504g at 08:53, `HotCorners: overview: hyprexpo is not
  loaded, the corner is off`) had no top-left corner until a config reload
  told it otherwise. The overview is the shell's now, so its corner is
  mapped like any other.

The corner toggles the overview (niri's), with the arming rule it already
had: it fires again only after a leave and the 400ms cooldown.

## 2. What it looks like

- One `formalshell:overview` layer on the Top layer of the focused output,
  anchored to every edge with exclusive zone 0, so the bar and the sidebar
  keep their band and stay usable over it, and every hot corner in that
  band stays reachable.
- The backdrop is the theme's `background` at full opacity. Each workspace
  of the output is a card the output's aspect at `zoom` (0.5, niri's
  default) of the area's size, filled with that output's own wallpaper
  picture (the one the shell already draws) at the cover radius, with a
  1px `border`; the selected workspace's border is `ring`.
- Workspaces stack top to bottom by index with `space.xl` between them,
  plus one empty card under the last: the slot a window dropped there
  makes a new workspace in. The selected one is centred vertically; the
  stack glides to the next centre on `arrive`'s clock.
- Inside a card every window sits at its own rect relative to the output,
  times the zoom, so a scrolling tape's columns keep their order and the
  ones past the output's edge run out past the card (clipped to the
  surface, as niri's strip does). A row slides sideways so its selected
  window is whole on screen. Floating windows draw last.
- A window is its captured frame at the cover radius with a 1px `border`,
  `ring` on the selected one. One with no frame yet (or one Hyprland will
  not copy: a scrolling column off its monitor) is a `cell` box with its
  app icon in the middle.
- The workspace's number sits over the card's top-left corner in the
  table's caption face; nothing else is written on it.

## 3. Motion

- **Open.** The surface maps fully transparent (the first frame inside
  50ms of the request on g815) and asks for the current workspace's frames
  at their full size. Once they land, or 150ms on, the zoom runs on
  `arrive`'s clock and curve: the current workspace from scale 1 at the
  output's own box down to its card, the others riding along, the
  backdrop and every other card fading in. A frame of the current
  workspace drawn at scale 1 sits on the live window under it, so the
  zoom starts on the desktop itself.
- **Close.** The selection is focused (the window, or the workspace when
  it has none) as the close starts, and the zoom runs back to scale 1 on
  the selected workspace, the backdrop fading out; the layer unmaps at
  the end, over the desktop it just matched.
- Every frame of either runs off the output's frame callbacks at its
  refresh. At rest nothing commits: no capture is live, and a frame lands
  only once per window per open (or again when a drop moves one).
- `motion.enabled: false` cuts both straight to their ends.

## 4. Input

The layer holds the keyboard (Exclusive) while open.

- Up and Down (and `k`/`j`) move the selection between workspaces; Left and
  Right (`h`/`l`) between the windows of one, in layout order (x, then y);
  Home and End to the row's first and last.
- A wheel notch moves one workspace; a sideways notch or Shift with the
  wheel moves one window.
- Return focuses the selection and closes. Escape, Super+O (the
  compositor's bind, `workspaces overview toggle`) and the corner close it
  the same way: like niri, where moving around the overview is moving
  focus, closing lands where the selection is.
- A click on a window focuses it and closes; on a card's empty part, that
  workspace; on the backdrop, closes on the selection.
- A press on a window that travels 6px lifts it: its frame follows the
  pointer at the zoom, the card under it takes the `ring`, and the release
  moves it there (`MoveWindowTo`, the empty card a new workspace) and
  keeps the overview open. Let go over the backdrop, it moves nothing.

## 5. Entry points

`workspaces overview open|close|toggle` (unchanged names), Super+O in the
shipped Lua bound to that, the top-left corner's `overview` action, and the
minimap: a 500ms press on an icon, or an icon dragged out past the column,
opens it. A drag between the minimap's own rows stays the minimap's move.

## 6. Captures

The overview is the capture rule's fourth site (`Owner::Overview`): one
frame per window of the output's workspaces, at the size its card draws
it, held only while the overview is open and every frame, session and
source dropped when the close's zoom ends. `debug dump`'s `captures`
lists them under `overview`.

## 7. Status

`debug dump`'s `overview`: `{open, phase (opening|open|closing|closed),
zoom, output, selected: {workspace, window}, rows: [{workspace, idx, card:
rect, windows: [{id, rect, captured}]}], drag, opens}`, rects in surface
pixels. The leg reads it.

## 8. What goes

HyprExpo entirely: the fspec package, its patch, its load and binds in the
owner's hyprland.lua, binutils (only Hyprland's `nm` for plugin hooks
needed it), the shell's plugin detection (`Command::Overview`, the
`plugin list` probe, `lua::overview`) and the `--overview` leg's plugin
path. The leg is rewritten for this surface and runs on both rigs.
