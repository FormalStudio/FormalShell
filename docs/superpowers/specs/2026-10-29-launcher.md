# The launcher

Owner, 2026-10-29 (`Plan.md`, decision 13 and phase 2). The launcher keeps
its surface, its routes and its IPC, and changes how it reads and how it is
driven. It is built on the one-look spec
(`2026-10-29-one-look.md`): its radii, type ramp, spacing grid and role
table hold here unless this spec names a launcher token. The approved
picture is `artifacts/plan/launcher.html`. This spec wins over DESIGN.md's
"Launcher" paragraph, its §4 "Keyboard" line on the launcher, and USAGE.md's
"Menu" section where they disagree; each task fixes the lines it makes
false.

The references: Pond's launcher and clipboard (one field, a list, a preview
pane only when there is something to preview; its serif is rejected, the
field is sans), and Tuna (subject, then action, then target; Tab shows what
a result can do; → steps into it; abbreviations it learns from what you
pick).

**Standing order.** Everything the launcher does today still happens after
this phase: every route, prefix, IPC verb and line of
`crates/formalshell-rs/tests/ipc-golden.jsonl`, every keyboard path, the
`--menu-budget` numbers. Where this spec moves a thing, §9 names where it
went.

## 0. Words

- **Subject**: one result row or cell. An app, a window, a route, a toggle,
  a network, a clipboard entry, an emoji, a file, a package, a process.
- **Action**: something done to a subject. The first one is the default
  and runs on Enter or a click.
- **Target**: the argument an action asks for (the workspace in "Move to
  workspace", the peer in "Send to"). Picked from a list like a subject.
- **Into**: a subject's own children (an app's windows, a folder's files, a
  network's details), reached with →.
- **Mode**: a route standing in the field as a token, scoping the list to
  its own subjects.

## 1. The opened state

### Layout

One card, centred, its top at 30% of the output (unchanged, `S:1763`, where
`S` is `crates/formalshell-rs/src/surfaces/launcher.rs`). Its composition,
spacing and sizes are Pond's (the owner's liked screenshots of 2026-10-30,
`artifacts/pond/liked/`, and its launcher, the vicinae fork
`bflycomputer/vicinae`); its colours, radii, type ramp and spacing grid are
the one-look table's; its field is sans. Three bands:

1. **Field.** A band `LAUNCHER.field_band` 64 tall whatever stands in it.
   The query draws in the sans face at 26px (`LAUNCHER.field_size`, a
   ratio of 2.0 on the 13px base, Pond's size), weight 400, `xxl` 24 in
   from either side. The placeholder reads `Search apps, files, commands`
   in `mutedForeground` at the root and `Search` inside an Into level. A
   mode token, when there is one, sits before the query (§4). Lucide
   `search` sits at the band's far end in `dim`, a mark rather than a
   button; click-outside and Escape close. A `separator` rule closes the
   band, `xxl` short of either edge. The caret is 2px of `primary` and
   still: a blink is a clock, and the open launcher commits nothing at
   rest.
2. **Body.** At rest the tiles (below); under a query or in a mode, rows
   with an `sm` 6 inset. Rows are 48px (`LAUNCHER.row_height`, libadwaita's
   row less 2), with a 24px icon (`LAUNCHER.row_icon`), `lg` 12 padding
   either side, `lg` between icon and label, label in `body` 13 at 400,
   the selected row's label at 600 (both weights shaped while closed, so a
   move costs no shaping). A dim `bodySmall` value sits against the row's
   trailing edge: a count ("2 windows"), the route's chord (`Super Ctrl
   V`), a source (`fspec`) or a checkmark. Rows abut (rung 1).
3. **Foot.** Under every state but the tiles: a full-bleed rule over it,
   then the selected subject's keys only, right-aligned, in `caption`
   `mutedForeground`, each a label and a keycap drawn as a glyph: `Open ↵`,
   `Shift ↵` naming the second action where there is one, `Actions ⇥`,
   `Into →` where the subject has children. The level name on the left
   (`S:2082-2131`) goes, because the mode token already names it. Every
   hint is a button (§6).

One width, `LAUNCHER.width` 640 (Pond's 638): a side column (actions or
preview, §5, §7) splits it, the list keeping 55% (Pond's 348 of 638)
behind a vertical `separator` rule `lg` short of the body's ends, rather
than widening the card. `popupWidthMenuApp` 900 stays for the monitor and
mirror views. The height follows the content: the card hugs its list,
never shorter than the opened state and never past
`LAUNCHER.height_share` 0.6 of the output; the clipboard routes and a
split body keep `popupHeightMenuSplit` 462 (Pond's 464). The top edge is
fixed, the bottom edge moving (USAGE "Getting around").

### What it lists

With an empty field at the root: `LAUNCHER.tiles` 3 tiles under no
heading, nothing else, as Pond's opened state. No section stack, no
shortcut chips, no rows.

- The tiles are the 3 best subjects in the picks ledger (§3), any kind: an
  app, a mode (Clipboard), an action row like `Shutdown`, a file, a
  toggle. An id that no longer resolves (an uninstalled app, a deleted
  file) is skipped and pruned on the next write.
- With fewer than 3 picks (a fresh profile) they are the first 3 root
  routes in declaration order, so a first open shows where things are.
- A tile is `LAUNCHER.tile_height` 144 tall, the three sharing the width
  `xxl` in from either side with `xl` 18 between them, `xl` under the
  field's rule and `xxl` under them. Its subject's icon at 24px sits `xl`
  in from its top-left, its name in `heading` 18 at 400 `xl` in from its
  bottom-left, `mutedForeground` until selected. Role `launcher.tile`.
- ← and → move between tiles, Enter runs the selected one, Tab opens its
  actions. Typing replaces them with rows. Every node still ranks under a
  query and every route is still a mode (§4), so nothing becomes
  unreachable.

### The app grid

`menu.appGrid` stays and defaults to **false** under the new look (today
true, `S:689`). The mockup's opened state is rows; a grid of 64px cells
cannot carry a mixed-kind Recent (a mode, a file and an action have no icon
worth 64px); four cells to a row at 640 put 8 apps in two rows and push
everything else under them; and a row list carries the trailing count and
chord the grid has no room for. A user `true` still wins and draws what it
draws today, adapted:

- Root, empty field: the 8 most-picked apps as cells under no heading,
  then `Recent` rows for the non-app picks.
- A query: app results as cells first, other results as rows under them
  (`appgrid::partition`, unchanged).
- Tab on a cell opens its actions; right-click does too. → and ← move
  cells on the grid, so Into is reached through the actions column there
  (`Show its windows →`).

## 2. Selection, hover and the row roles

The selection is one soft fill, `radiusMd` 9, travelling on `spatialFast`
as the cursor does today (DESIGN §1 "The cursor travels"); a wrap, a
filter reset and a level change snap. No ring, no leading bar
(`S:1914-1921`'s bar and brackets go). Hover is a second, lighter tint on
the row under the pointer, which never travels and fades on `effectsFast`;
it is separate from the selection, and the pointer never moves the
selection (§6).

One role in `crates/fs-theme/formalshell.json`, `launcher.row`, with
states `rest` (nothing drawn), `hover`, `selected`, and `selected` winning
over `hover` on the same row. Dark: `foreground` at 0.085 for `selected`,
0.045 for `hover` (the mockup's two values). Light: `foreground` at 0.07
and 0.035, read off `--gallery`'s light sheet and moved until the selected
row is plain against the hovered one at a glance. `launcher.token` is the
mode chip: `primary` at 0.16 fill, `primary` ink, `radiusSm` 6, label in
`caption` at 600. Keycaps keep the `keycap` role. `mutedForeground` on
`card` stays at or above 4.5:1 in both modes (DESIGN §6).

## 3. Ranking and abbreviations

### One ranked list

A query at the root ranks the whole tree, as today (`fs-menu/src/search.rs`,
`routeOnly` and `localOnly` kept), and draws the 40 best as **one list in
score order** under no heading. The grouping by root route
(`search.rs:166-179`) goes: it put a weaker hit of the leading route above
a stronger hit of the next, which is exactly what a learned abbreviation
has to beat. Each row's trailing value says what kind it is where the
label does not ("Application", "Command", a route's chord), as
`actions.rs:176-190` already does.

Two tiers join the scorer under the existing ones, so `ff` finds Firefox
before anything is learned:

| tier | match |
| --- | --- |
| configured or pinned abbreviation | the query is an abbreviation set for this subject |
| learned abbreviation | the query has taught this subject, and it dominates (below) |
| exact, starts with, contains, alias, title | today's five, in today's order |
| initials | the query is the initials of the label's words (`vsc`, Visual Studio Code) |
| subsequence | every query character appears in the label, in order (`ff`, Firefox) |

A hit's rank is a pair, compared tier first: `(tier, bonus)`. Today the two
are one number (`search.rs:15-21`, tiers 200 apart, `ROOT_BONUS` +100 and
`APP_DEMOTION` -50 folded in), which only works while every bonus stays
under the gap; two more tiers and a learned boost would cross it. The
bonus is `ROOT_BONUS` at the root, minus `APP_DEMOTION` for an app, plus
the learned boost below; then shallower depth, then frecency off the picks
ledger, then declaration order break ties, as today.

### The picks ledger

`state.json`'s `appLaunches` becomes `picks`: the same `frecency::Record`
(`{id, count, lastMs}`, 14-day half-life, best 200 kept), keyed by subject
id across every kind (`app:<desktop id>`, a node id, `file:<path>`,
`emoji:<char>`). Every pick records one. It feeds `Recent`, the tie-break
and the grid's 8 cells. `emojiUses` stays separate: emoji ranking is its own
six tiers (`providers/emoji.rs:114-149`) and an emoji grid ranks nothing
else. On load, an `appLaunches` array with no `picks` beside it is read
into `picks` (each id prefixed `app:`) and the old key is not written
again: state is the shell's own file, and this keeps a profile's app order
across the upgrade.

### Abbreviations

An abbreviation is a query (1 to 8 characters, lowercased, not starting
with `:`) that names a subject. There are three sources:

1. **Configured**, in `settings.json`, which the shell only reads:

   ```jsonc
   { "menu": { "abbreviations": { "ff": "app:firefox.desktop", "wf": "wifi" } } }
   ```

2. **Pinned from the launcher**, in `state.json` under
   `abbreviations.pinned`, the same shape: the `Pin "ff"` and `Edit
   abbreviation` actions write it (§5).
3. **Learned**, in `state.json` under `abbreviations.learned`: a map from
   query to frecency records. Picking a subject while the field holds a
   query of 1 to 8 characters records `(query, subject)`, with the same
   14-day half-life. Nothing is learned in Select or Input mode, inside the
   Install mode's search (a package is not yet a subject anyone uses), or
   from a query that matched the subject at the exact tier already (typing
   `firefox` teaches nothing new).

Which one wins, for one query:

- Configured beats pinned beats learned. A configured abbreviation is the
  declarative one and the launcher cannot change it, so a pin in
  `state.json` for the same query is ignored while the configured one
  exists (the Plan's open question on a settings UI proposes the same
  order: settings.json pins, state.json overrides only what it leaves
  free).
- A learned subject takes the learned tier only when it **dominates**:
  its record's `count` is at least 2, its decayed score is at least 1 (so
  two picks dominate for the 14 days of one half-life after the last of
  them, and a pick a fortnight renews it), and that score is at least 60%
  of all decayed learned score for the query. Below that, its decayed
  score is added to the bonus (`min(150, 50 × score)`), never the tier, so
  a query you have used for two different things ranks both higher
  without either jumping.
- A pinned or configured subject that is not currently resolvable (the app
  is gone) does not hide the rest; the query ranks as if it had none, and
  the abbreviation stays until forgotten.

Decay and size: a learned record whose score falls under 0.1 is dropped on
the next write (about 47 days after a single pick); the map keeps the 500
best `(query, subject)` pairs. Pinned and configured abbreviations never
decay.

Unlearning: a row that leads because of an abbreviation shows it as its
trailing value (`ff`, in `monospace`, a pin icon beside a pinned one), and
its actions (§5) carry `Forget "ff"` for a learned or pinned one, and
`Edit abbreviation` and `Pin "ff"` for a learned one. Forgetting a learned
pair deletes it; the next pick of the same pair starts it again from one,
which needs a second pick before it dominates. A configured abbreviation
carries neither and reads `Set in settings.json` in the actions column.
The `Abbreviations` mode (§4) lists all three sources with the same
actions.

## 4. Modes

A mode is a route standing in the field as a token: `[Clipboard] query`.
The list then holds that route's subjects only, and the query searches
inside it. Today a query inside a level still ranks the whole tree
(`S:754`); inside a mode it ranks the mode's subtree (`rank(..)` from the
mode's node rather than the roots), which is what a mode is for. The
special views keep their own search (emoji's tiers, keybinds', the
picker's filename filter, the monitor's process filter).

### Getting in and out

- **In**: Enter or a click on a mode's row (the row's default action is
  `Open`); → on it; its prefix and a space (`:e `, `:k `, `:r `, `:i `),
  which turns into the token and keeps what follows as the query; `menu
  summon <id>` and every shipped chord, unchanged; a `:`-led summon, as
  today (`S:1191`).
- **Nesting**: a mode inside a mode (System, then Power) shows one token
  carrying the path, `System › Power`, ancestors in `mutedForeground`;
  past two levels it shows `… › ` and the last two.
- **→ and ←**: → steps into the selected subject only when all of these
  hold: Menu mode (never Select or Input), the list has the keys (not the
  column), no modifier is held, the caret is at the end of the query (an
  empty query included), the subject has Into, and the view is a row list.
  Anything else keeps today's dispatch (`nav.rs:261-326`): a grid, the
  picker and emoji move a cell; the field moves the caret, Ctrl a word,
  Shift extends; a subject with no Into leaves → to the caret, which is
  already at the end and stays. ← with an empty query inside a mode or an
  Into level leaves one level, as Backspace does; with text it is the
  caret's. In the column, ← with an empty filter closes the column.
- **Out**: Backspace on an empty field leaves one level, on the first press
  only (a held key climbs exactly one, as `nav.rs:314-322` does today);
  Escape steps back one thing at a time (§5's column, then the query,
  then the mode, then the launcher); a click on the token leaves it.
- A mode with a `prefix` key in `menu.jsonc` gets it as its prefix; the
  shipped tree sets the four above. `ROUTE_PREFIXES` (`actions.rs:149`),
  which lists `:nix` and misses `:r`, is replaced by reading that key.

## 5. Actions

### The column

Tab, or a right-click on a row, opens the selected subject's actions in a
column beside the list: the list keeps the left 55% with the subject
still selected, the column takes the rest, the card's width unchanged. On a split route the
column replaces the preview pane while it is open.

- Typing filters the actions (the field's query is saved and comes back on
  the way out; the field shows `Actions` as a second token).
- ↑ ↓, Page and Home/End move inside the column; Enter or a click runs one.
- Shift+Tab, Escape or ← with an empty filter closes the column. A click
  on the list while the column is open closes it and selects the clicked
  row without running it; the next click runs it.
- An action that needs a target (`Move to workspace…`, `Send to…`) replaces
  the column's contents with the targets, a third token in the field
  (`Workspace`); Shift+Tab goes back to the actions.
- An action that needs words (`Edit abbreviation`, `Rename`) opens the
  Input step the launcher already has, its answer in process.
- The column scrolls on its own, with the same coast as the list.

Enter on the list runs the first action. **Shift+Enter runs the second**,
which is how every existing alternate keeps its key: `activateAlternate`
over IPC is the same call. The second slot belongs to the node's own
`alternate` wherever one exists (a provider's or a `menu.jsonc` entry's,
`node.rs:197-198`); the kind's generic actions follow it. An inert subject
(a dim note, a keybind, an unavailable row: what gets no primary verb
today, `actions.rs:5-7`) has no default: Enter, Shift+Enter and a click do
nothing on it, as today, and Tab still opens its column for the actions
it does have. Arm-then-confirm stays a property of the action
(`confirm: true`), so `Shutdown` still needs two Enters from the list and
two from the column.

### The model, as data

The action list is data in `fs-menu`, built per subject on Tab and never
per keystroke:

```rust
pub struct Action {
    pub id: String,             // "open", "new-window", "move-to-workspace"
    pub label: String,
    pub icon: Option<String>,
    pub run: Run,               // Command(String) | Ipc(String) | Into(String) | Target(TargetKind) | Input(String)
    pub confirm: bool,
    pub keep_open: bool,
    pub destructive: bool,      // drawn with a `destructive` icon, never a filled row
}
```

The list for a subject is, in order: the kind's built-in actions
(`fs-menu/src/actions.rs` gains `actions_for(&Subject) -> Vec<Action>`),
then the subject node's own `actions` array from `menu.jsonc` (a new key,
each entry `{id, label, icon?, action, confirm?, keepOpen?}`, merged per id
like every other key), then the abbreviation actions (§3), then actions
from launcher plugins (§10). A `menu.jsonc` entry with a built-in's id
overrides it; `"hidden": true` on one removes it. The default action is
whichever comes first after that.

Each kind keeps its current Enter and Shift+Enter: saved Wi-Fi's Forget,
a radio favourite's Remove favourite, a clipboard image's Send over SSH
(`W:270-288`, which keeps its fallback of opening `clipssh` when no alias
resolves). The app row's Shift+Enter becomes New window (USAGE promised
the discrete GPU, which was never wired; it is an action in the column
now).

## 6. Mouse

For eating with one hand. Every claim here is a leg (§12).

- **Hover**: the `hover` tint on the row or cell under the pointer, in the
  list and the column. It never moves the selection, so the foot keeps
  naming the selected subject and Enter still runs it. Today's "hover
  moves the cursor when the pointer is what moved" (`W:984-995`) goes.
- **Click**: press and release on the same row selects it and runs its
  first action. A click on a mode row enters the mode. With the actions
  column open, a click on the list only closes the column and selects
  (§5). A click on an inert subject selects it and runs nothing.
- **Right-click**: selects the row and opens its actions (§5). Today any
  button is a left click (`W:1001-1009`); the button is now read.
- **Foot hints**: buttons. A click on `Actions ⇥` is Tab, on `Into →` is →,
  on `Open ↵` is Enter, each with its own hover tint.
- **Token**: a click on a mode token leaves that mode (a nested token's
  ancestor leaves to it).
- **Outside**: a press and release outside the card close it, as today.
- **Wheel and touchpad**: `scroll.rs`, unchanged (one notch a row or a
  grid row, 1.12 finger gain, rubber band, coast, bounce); the column has
  its own scroll state. Scrolling never moves the selection or the hover
  tint's subject until the pointer moves.

After a pointer has been used, the first arrow key moves from the
selection, never from the hovered row.

## 7. Preview pane and files

The pane shows only when the selected subject has a preview, taking the
body's right 45% behind its rule (§1) at the card's one width, and a
subject without a preview gives the list its whole width back, whatever
route it sits in; the card's height morphs on `spatial`, a retargeting
tween, so a selection crossing mixed rows quickly glides rather than
snapping. As Pond's clipboard draws it, the pane is no card of its own:
the preview at its top, the subject's name in `body` at 500 and its facts
in `caption` `mutedForeground` at its foot, `lg` in from every side.

| subject | preview |
| --- | --- |
| clipboard text | the text, wrapped and clamped, with its capture time |
| clipboard image | the image at true colour (`S:2445-2449`), its size and time |
| file, image | a thumbnail through `fs-menu/src/thumbnails.rs`, then name, size, modified |
| file, text | its first 40 lines in `monospace`, read on the service thread, 64 KB at most |
| folder | its entry count and the first 12 names |
| window | the window's last frame, its title and workspace |
| package | the catalogue's description, source, version, size (§8) |

**Windows** need a capture, and the capture rule (CLAUDE.md) allows it in
exactly two places. This spec asks for a third, on the same terms: the
pane captures the one selected window through `wayland/capture.rs` while
the pane shows it, one frame and no live stream, dropping the frame,
session and source when the selection moves or the card closes; the
switcher's own release rule, checked the same way. The rule's text in
CLAUDE.md and DESIGN §5 gains the launcher's pane in task 2.7, and no
other surface. The owner approved this third place on 2026-10-29.

**Files** are a new source with no index: the GTK recent-files list
(`$XDG_DATA_HOME/recently-used.xbel`, the freedesktop place every GTK and
most Qt apps write) and the XDG user directories (`xdg-user-dir`'s
Documents, Downloads, Pictures, ...) rank in the root search as file and
folder subjects; → on a folder lists it (`read_dir` on the service thread,
folders first, 500 entries at most, hidden files only after typing `.`).
The `Files` mode is the same list alone. A whole-disk search (plocate,
tracker) is out of this phase: it is a new runtime dependency under the
"everything ships" rule and wants its own design.

## 8. Install mode

The fspec side (catalogue search, how an install lands in the declarative
spec, privileges, the operation stream) is
`~/Developer/ecosystem/FormalSpec/docs/shell-install.md`. This section is
the launcher's half and names only what it needs from that doc:

1. a search call returning, per package, an id, a name, a source, a
   version, an icon name or path, a description and whether it is
   installed;
2. an install call and an uninstall call taking a package id and returning
   an operation id at once;
3. the operation stream of Plan phase 3.2 (operation id, phase, progress,
   result), which Activity (3.3) already reads;
4. an owner query: which fspec package, if any, put a given desktop file
   there.

The launcher never runs a package manager itself and never asks for a
password itself; privilege prompts are the shell's polkit agent's, as for
everything else.

**Searching.** `Install` is a mode (`:i `, or its name). Its query is sent
to the search call on the service thread, debounced 250ms, at 2 characters
or more, a new query cancelling the one in flight. Rows: the package's
icon at 22px (an icon name through the theme, else a path, else the
`package` icon), its name, and a trailing `bodySmall` of source and
version; an installed package is ticked and reads `Installed`. Every state
is one dim row: `Searching…`, `No packages for "x"`, a failed search with
its reason, and `Installing apps needs fspec` with no fspec on PATH. The
preview pane shows the description.

**Installing.** Enter (`Install`) calls install and leaves the launcher
open on the row, its icon slot replaced by a progress ring off the
operation stream and its trailing value by the phase (`Downloading 40%`).
Activity takes over once the launcher closes: Pond's "Installing Firefox"
ring, one row per operation (Plan decision 12). A failure puts the reason
on the row and in Activity; the row goes back to Install.

**Run once.** A row fspec marks `run` and that is not installed carries
Run once after Install (Tab, or Shift+Enter): `fspec run --detach <id>`,
the launcher closing, the run followed in Activity like any `fspec run`.

**Landing.** The stream's success makes the shell rescan desktop entries
(`Ask::Apps`, `W:1087`), records a pick for the new app (count 1, so it
heads `Recent` once and then decays like anything else), and Activity's
finished row carries `Open`. A search for it now finds the app subject
above the package.

**Uninstall.** An app row's last action, present only when the owner query
names an fspec package for its desktop file (a distro's base apps carry
none). It confirms like any destructive action (two Enters, the row's
label reading `Uninstall Firefox?`), closes the launcher, and runs in
Activity the same way. Success rescans; the app's picks and abbreviations
are pruned as unresolvable on the next write.

## 10. Routes and plugins contribute

- **Routes** contribute subjects as today (providers, `menu.jsonc` nodes)
  and actions through a node's `actions` key (§5). A route's provider may
  also attach actions to its own subjects from Rust (`Node.actions`, the
  field `actions_for` reads after the kind's built-ins).
- **Plugins** gain a fifth manifest kind, `launcher`, and two keys, `mode`
  (a display name; the plugin becomes a mode with id `plugin:<id>`) and
  `subjects` (subject kinds it adds actions to, e.g. `["file", "app"]`).
  Protocol, JSON lines as for every plugin: the shell sends
  `{"event":"query","text":"..."}` while its mode is open and the plugin
  answers `{"rows":[...]}` (the row objects USAGE already defines, plus
  optional `actions`); on Tab over a subject of a declared kind the shell
  sends `{"event":"actions","subject":{"id":"...","kind":"...","path":"..."}}`
  and the plugin answers `{"actions":[{"id","label","icon"}]}`; running one
  sends `{"event":"activate","id":"...","action":"..."}`. A launcher plugin
  runs only while the launcher is open, like a non-`keepLoaded` panel. Its
  actions join the column when they arrive, faded in on `effects` under
  the built-ins, never moving the selected action; one that has not
  answered in 300ms is left out of that opening.

## 11. Motion, layout, overflow, budgets

**Motion.** Opening and closing are `Card`'s, unchanged: under
`motion.morph: true` the card buds off the layout's line and lets go of
it; under `false` it drops as a popover. The side column's arrival is the
width morph on `spatial` with the column's content on one `effects` fade
in, as a level change already plays. Rows re-rank through `RowMotion`
(`S:148-213`), unchanged. The selection travels on `spatialFast`, the
hover tint fades on `effectsFast` and never moves (DESIGN §5), the mode
token widens from nothing on `spatialFast` while the query text slides
over by the same clock. `motion.enabled: false` zeroes all of it.

**Where it buds from.** With the bar on the top edge, the bar's line, as
today. With the bar on another edge, the output's top edge, as today.
Under `layout: sidebar` (Plan phase 4) the sidebar's inner edge is the
line: the card rests centred both ways in the area the sidebar leaves,
and the join derives the depth back to that vertical
line the way `--bar-position left --join` already does for panels.

**Overflow.**

- Long names: one line, elided at the end; the trailing value takes at
  most 40% of the row and elides first. Never a marquee.
- Long lists: the list scrolls under the 0.6 height cap; `Recent` holds 8.
- Many actions: the column scrolls on its own; past 8 actions the filter is
  the expected way in, and the column's last visible row reads `n more`
  when the list runs past it.
- Deep modes: the token keeps the last two levels behind `…`.
- Long queries: the field scrolls to keep the caret in view.
- A narrow output: the card is at most the output minus `screenPadding`
  either side, and the side column 45% of it; at a 1280px output the 640
  card fits as is.
- Installs: one row per operation in Install mode, ten at once included.

**Budgets.** The `--menu-budget` numbers stand as they are: the first
launcher commit at most 50ms after `menu toggle`, the shell's hold on a
bar frame at most 16ms, no bar gap over 33ms, and no launcher commit in
five seconds open at rest. Three more rests join that last claim: with the
actions column open, with the preview pane showing an image, and with a
window preview showing. All of it is read plain and under
`FS_CPU_QUOTA=10%`, and against the one-look baseline gate
(`2026-10-29-one-look.md` §5) with RSS at most 2 MB over. What keeps them:

- `Recent` and its icons are resolved while closed, as root rows are today
  (`W:834-843`).
- Abbreviation lookup is one hash lookup per keystroke; the learned map is
  500 pairs at most.
- The action list is built on Tab, never per keystroke; the foot needs
  only the first two actions and whether Into exists, a table lookup.
- Files, thumbnails, text previews, Install's search and plugin queries all
  run off the main thread and land as damage; none blocks a frame.
- A window preview holds one frame, not a stream.
