# The seams

Owner, 2026-10-30 (`Plan.md` phase 6): power-on to desktop is one
continuous picture, and the way out mirrors it. Owned across this repo
(the greeter, the shell's arrival and departure, the Hyprland include,
`dev/seams.sh`) and the owner's fspec spec (`modules/boot/`, the greetd
session script, `hosts/fstestvm`). Verified only on the VM rig's film.

## 1. What the film showed (2026-10-29)

`dev/seams.sh`, frame n at n x 100 ms:

| leg | frames | what is on screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| boot | 0 to 6 | QEMU's "Guest has not initialized the display", then black |
| boot | 7 to 48 | Limine's text menu with its countdown and comment line |
| boot | 49 to 60 | black (the kernel and initrd load) |
| boot | 61 to 89 | the splash: logo, name, progress bar |
| boot | 90 to 93 | black: plymouth quit, sway starting |
| boot | 94 to 175 | the greeter, a cursor in its top left |
| boot | 176 to 185 | black, then uwsm's stdout in green on VT1, black again |
| boot | 186 on | the desktop: bar, no wallpaper, Hyprland's own cursor mid-screen |
| logout | 10 to 13 | uwsm's stdout on VT1, then black, then the greeter |
| shutdown | 52 to 70 | the desktop cut to the systemd console, "The system will power off now!" |

Every text frame is VT1 drawn by fbcon: uwsm's stdout (greetd gives the
session the VT as its terminal), and systemd's stop messages, which
`quiet` alone does not silence once a stop job is slow
(`systemd.show_status=auto`). The shutdown splash never showed: the
session held the display until systemd was already printing.

## 2. The picture, stage by stage

The rule a frame is read against: from the splash's first frame to the
desktop, and from the shell's departure to power-off, a frame carries only
our pictures. A stage hands over in one of two ways, and nothing else:

- **Same pixels.** The next stage's first frame is the last one's
  picture, so the cut is invisible.
- **Through black, as motion.** The outgoing stage fades to black, the
  incoming one fades in from it. Black held between the two fades is
  bounded: 1.5 s on the VM rig, where two compositors hand over.

Before the splash the firmware owns the screen (QEMU's placeholder, a
vendor logo on the laptops): no text of ours, and black between Limine and
the kernel is the firmware's.

### Boot

1. **Limine**: `quiet: yes`, `timeout: 1`. Nothing drawn; any key during
   the second shows the themed menu as today (the Kernels submenu, g815's
   Windows entry). systemd-boot behind it already shows nothing.
2. **Kernel**: one shared argument list in `modules/boot/boot.star`
   (`QUIET_ARGS`), the hosts appending only their own:
   `quiet splash loglevel=3 rd.udev.log_level=3 udev.log_level=3
   systemd.show_status=false rd.systemd.show_status=false
   vt.global_cursor_default=0 fbcon=vc:2-6`. `fbcon=vc:2-6` leaves VT1,
   greetd's, on the dummy console: nothing written to it is ever drawn,
   which is what kills uwsm's stdout and the stop messages for good. tty2
   still carries a getty on Ctrl+Alt+F2.
3. **Splash**: the logo and name fade in over 300 ms (plymouth's refresh
   callback) instead of cutting in; the bar is unchanged. A status
   `formalos-done` (from `plymouth update`) fills the bar.
4. **Splash to greeter, same pixels.** `plymouth-quit.service` sends
   `formalos-done`, waits 150 ms, then quits with `--retain-splash`;
   greetd starts after it. formalos-boot renders the splash's finished
   frame (logo, name, full bar, on black) at each of the script's four
   scales as `greeter-<s>.png`, laid out on the script's own grid around
   the canvas centre. The greeter session script picks `<s>` off the
   connected output's preferred mode exactly as the script does
   (`height / 500`, 1 to 4) and gives it to sway as `bg ... center
   #000000`, only for the first greeter since boot (a marker in
   `/run/formalshell-greeter`); a greeter after a logout gets plain black.
   The one black left here is sway's own: its first frames after the
   modeset are its clear colour, before swaybg commits the replica (three
   frames, 60 ms, on the VM rig). Removing them takes a patched sway.
5. **Greeter**: its surfaces commit at opacity 0 and rise to 1 over
   500 ms on the `effects` curve once the backdrop has decoded (or 1 s
   has passed), so the card and wallpaper dissolve in over the splash.
   The pointer is hidden on the greeter's surfaces until it first moves.
6. **Greeter to session, through black.** When greetd accepts the start,
   a `formalshell:greeter-veil` overlay fades in to opaque black over
   300 ms and the greeter exits on the frame after it lands.
   The black after it is held, not drawn, and bounded by the 1.5 s above
   (measured 1.3 s on the VM rig, 2026-10-30): once sway exits the CRTC
   goes dark at once, and logind takes the greeter's DRM device as its
   session closes, so no greeter process can keep a picture up across the
   gap, and the guest has no `/dev/fb0` to leave one on. About 0.9 s of
   it has no compositor on the screen (uwsm, then Hyprland's own start),
   the last 0.4 s is Hyprland's `background_color` before the shell's
   first commit. Closing it means one compositor across login (autologin into
   the session with the shell's lock up first), a change to the login
   architecture rather than to a stage.
7. **Session arrival.** Hyprland starts on black
   (`misc.background_color = 0x000000`) with `cursor.invisible = true`
   (the include and the VM's config). The shell, on the first start for
   this Hyprland instance (no marker `$XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/formalshell/arrived-<signature>` yet),
   maps an opaque `formalshell:veil` per output above everything before
   any other surface, builds the bar and the wallpaper under it, and fades
   it out over 500 ms once both have committed (cap 2 s from start). On
   the fade's last frame it writes the marker and shows the pointer. The
   marker comes only then: the shell's first theme publish runs `hyprctl
   reload` mid-arrival, and the config hides the pointer for as long as the
   marker is absent. Hidden twice over, since Hyprland 0.56 draws it for its
   first frames whatever `cursor.invisible` says: the config also starts
   Hyprland on `formalos-blank`, an Xcursor theme of one transparent pixel it
   writes under `~/.local/share/icons` (`XCURSOR_THEME`, read once by the
   cursor manager, goes back to the real theme on `hyprland.start`, before
   anything else starts; the installer's entry applies the blank again after
   `hypr-user.lua`), and defines `formalshell_arrived()`,
   which the shell's reveal calls through eval: `XCURSOR_THEME` back, the
   real theme through `hyprctl setcursor` (0.56 has no Lua call for it),
   `cursor.invisible = false`. The include
   also runs `sleep 10` then the marker and `formalshell_arrived()`, so a shell that never starts
   leaves a visible cursor. A restart of the shell inside the session (the
   watchdog, an update) finds the marker and maps no veil.

### Way out

8. **Departure**: one path for logout, reboot and shutdown. The shell
   holds a logind `delay` inhibitor on `shutdown` (as `services/sleep.rs`
   does on `sleep`); on `PrepareForShutdown(true)` it fades the veil in
   over 300 ms, hides the cursor and releases the inhibitor on the frame
   after it lands (cap 1 s). So `systemctl poweroff` from a terminal
   leaves the same way the launcher does. Logout is `power.logout` (IPC
   and the launcher's `system.logout`): the same fade, then
   `compositor::Command::Exit` (Hyprland `hl.dsp.exit()`, mango's `quit`).
9. **After the session**: Hyprland exits on black; VT1 is the dummy
   console, so the screen stays black until plymouth's shutdown splash
   (`plymouth-poweroff`/`-reboot`, already static units pulled by their
   targets), which fades its logo and name in like the boot. Reboot then
   runs the boot again; logout reaches a black greeter that fades in.

### Unlock

10. **Lock to desktop, same pixels.** On PAM's success each output's last
    lock picture moves onto a `formalshell:veil` overlay, committed while
    the session is still locked (cap 300 ms, then the unlock goes on
    without the ones still to come). The session unlocks once every one
    is committed, so the compositor's first unlocked frame is the lock's
    picture over the desktop, and the veils fade off it over the
    `effects` clock. Proven by `--unlock`.

## 3. What the film proves

`dev/seams.sh` keeps filming the four legs, then `dev/seams-check.py`
reads every frame and writes `artifacts/seams/<leg>-sheet.png` (one frame
per change, numbered) and `artifacts/seams/report.txt`, failing on:

- **text**: tesseract finds words on a frame that is not the greeter or
  the desktop (those carry our own text). Run from the splash's first
  frame to the greeter, and from the departure's first black frame to the
  end of the leg.
- **held black**: a run of black frames (mean luminance under 1) between
  two stages longer than 1.5 s, or a black frame not reached by a fade
  (the frame before it already under half of the stage's luminance).
- **jump**: a picture change where the previous and next stage share
  neither pixels nor a monotone luminance ramp.

Frames before the first non-black, non-firmware frame are reported, not
judged.

## 4. The rest of phase 6

- **First run with no settings.json**: the arrival above with no
  settings file: the bar on its defaults, the wallpaper's fallback colour,
  nothing logged as an error. Read off the seams boot leg (the VM user has
  none).
- **Launch feedback**: a desktop entry the shell spawns (the launcher's
  rows and grid, the app menu, Activity's open, `monitor launch`, and
  `activity launch <id>` for a keybind) is a running `launch:<id>` item
  in Activity, the bar's ring spinning, until a window of it maps that it
  did not have at the spawn (`services/launch.rs`, matched as the
  launcher's own `appmatch`). None in 15 s reads failed ("<name> has not
  opened a window") until seen, and a window mapping later still takes it
  back. No toast card: the ring is the feedback. Proven by `--launch`.
- **Unavailable states**: the VM has no battery, no Bluetooth controller
  and no camera; the desktop frame after arrival is read for the honest
  cells, never an invented device.
